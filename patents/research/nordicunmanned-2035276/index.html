Research examplesEP 2 035 276 B1

Aerospace · Multirotor aircraft

Two claims, two charts, one invention.

Counter-rotating coaxial rotors on individually speed-controlled electric motors, charted against a 2005 quadrotor testbed and a 1991 coaxial model craft.

Nordic UnmannedEP 2 035 276 B1 · Claim 1Submitted research

The technical question

Which earlier aircraft already combined three or more drive units, each with two counter-rotating rotors on their own speed-controlled electric motors, under autonomous attitude and position control?

The research approach

The research charted the European patent and its American sibling separately, each from its own claim language. It followed the drive unit from radio-controlled coaxial models through ducted-fan designs to a university quadrotor testbed, and kept the German claim’s wording apart from the English chart that reads it.

Two claims, two charts

Chart each claim on its own words and keep the weakest limitation of each in view.

Limitation 1f · B 80 · Weakest

Every motor’s speed can be varied individually.

STARMAC computes a net thrust value for each rotor and drives each motor by its own output; within the pair, Kinoshita rotates each propeller at a speed independent of the other. The claim asks for the capability, which these documents state.

Passages on the original page

The control inputs for each rotor corresponding to pitch, roll and yaw are computed separately, and then added to the altitude input and the nominal thrust to yield the net thrust value for each rotor.

R10-DASC · The Stanford Testbed of Autonomous Rotorcraft for Multi Agent Control (STARMAC) · Printed p. 12.E.4-8

What the score reflects

The weakest limitation. The owner will cite craft that run a unit’s two rotors together, at equal speeds or on one motor. Each is another design or an option beside the independent drive; none says a unit’s motors should not have individually variable speeds.

R10-DASC · Printed p. 12.E.4-8 · R2 · Claim 1, column 5, lines 44–45

Recorded assessment: 18 October 2026. Scores assess the evidence for this ground; they are not probabilities of invalidity. P1 is the reason to combine, a claim-wide point. The claim’s authentic text is German; the chart’s English reading is shown.

Preamble 1pre · A 98

The STARMAC paper describes a four-rotor aircraft with fixed-pitch blades, a quadrotor, flown autonomously outdoors. The claim’s German word, “Fluggerät”, is read as “aircraft”.

The words are plain. The date rests on the conference proceedings of August 2005, ten months before the priority date.

R10 · STARMAC, IEEE/RSJ IROS 2005 · Printed p. 468

R10 · Original p. 468

Limitation 1a · A 96

The companion STARMAC paper mounts four fixed-pitch rotors at the ends of a cross frame, each rotor on its own motor: four drive units where the claim asks for at least three.

The owner may read “drive unit” as the assembly that carries both rotors. That reading goes to limitation 1d, where each unit gains its second rotor; for the count, STARMAC’s four rotor-and-motor units are the units.

R10-DASC · STARMAC, DASC 2004 · Printed p. 12.E.4-1

R10-DASC · Printed p. 12.E.4-1

Limitation 1b · A 94

The on-board controller drives each motor through its own pulse-width-modulated output, and each rotor’s thrust follows the voltage applied to its motor. Each unit therefore has a first rotor on an electric motor whose speed is set.

The owner can argue that a voltage-commanded motor is not “speed-controlled”. The claim’s own words settle it: “gesteuert” (set) stands beside the closed-loop “Regelung” of limitation 1c.

R10-DASC · Printed p. 12.E.4-4

R10-DASC · Printed p. 12.E.4-4

Limitation 1c · B 87

STARMAC flew autonomous hover with closed-loop control of attitude, altitude and position: inner loops through the motor voltages, an outer position loop fed by GPS. Its companion paper puts the controller on board the craft.

The two papers describe one testbed by one group and are scored as two references. Neither says in so many words where the outer, horizontal position loop runs; the answer is the companion paper’s “all control functions onboard”.

R10 · Printed pp. 468, 470 · R10-DASC · Printed p. 12.E.4-8

R10 · Original p. 468 R10 · Original p. 470 R10-DASC · Printed p. 12.E.4-8

Limitation 1d · B 82

Kinoshita gives each rotor of a coaxial pair its own variable-speed motor, the two propellers turning in opposite directions. Given that unit, each of STARMAC’s drive units has a second rotor on a second speed-controlled electric motor.

The rating rests on P1, the reason to fit Kinoshita’s unit to each of STARMAC’s units. The structural reading of “drive unit” is met, since Kinoshita’s pair is one coaxial assembly.

Limitation 1e · B 82

Kinoshita’s two propellers rotate in opposite directions, each at its own speed; Pauli’s upper and lower propellers are driven to opposite rotation independently of each other.

STARMAC turns rotors of different units opposite to each other, not the two rotors of one unit. The limitation comes with the unit of 1d and rests on P1, with no open point of its own.

Claim-wide point P1 · B 82

The reason to give each of STARMAC’s four units a second, counter-rotating rotor on its own speed-controlled electric motor, Kinoshita’s unit, for the patent’s own problem: a more compact craft for the same payload that keeps flying when a rotor fails. The art states the reasons, and Spirov and Pedersen had already put a counter-rotating pair in each of four units.

Two references in the same field with a reason stated in the art would rate higher; the point is held at 82 for the owner’s two answers, a remark that multi-rotor craft are harder to balance and a hindsight argument. This point affects the 1d–1f ratings.

Passages on the original page

If the information supplied by the sensor (61) indicates that the aircraft is tilting towards the right side, then the correction consists in increasing the speed of the propeller 11, decreasing the speed of the propeller 13, meanwhile the speeds of the propellers 10 and 12 remain unchanged.

R1 · Louvel, US 2002/0104921 A1 · Printed p. 4

What the score reflects

The weakest limitation. No reference controls attitude and position through the individual speeds of both motors in each of three or more coaxial units, so the loop over eight motors is the combination’s (W2), and the narrow reading of “attitude and position control” (W1) carries over from 1a.

Preamble 1pre · A 98

Louvel describes a remotely controlled, remotely supplied aircraft powered by propellers driven by electric motors, able to hover and move in the three directions of space.

Literal and in plain words.

R1 · US 2002/0104921 A1 · [0007]

R1 · Original p. 1

Limitation 1a · C 66

Louvel’s on-board tilt sensors and yaw gyro feed a closed loop that holds the attitude, cancels yaw and moves the craft through its four motor units. The examiner read the term as automatic control by programmable electronics, as the patent itself does.

Under the owner’s narrow reading, closed-loop attitude and position control on board, Louvel’s loop runs off board and the craft is piloted through a handle; the chart then rests on STARMAC and Wobben. Scored at W1.

R1 · Original p. 2 R1 · Original p. 4

Limitation 1b · A 98

Louvel’s four lift propellers, one per drive unit; after the combination, the upper rotor of each coaxial pair.

Literal and in plain words.

R1 · [0029]

R1 · Original p. 1

Limitation 1c · A 94

Each propeller is driven independently by an electric motor, and the control unit sets the speed of each motor through its own pulse-width-modulated drive.

The owner can argue that a PWM current drive is not “rotary speed-controlled”; Louvel’s own words, “control the speed of each electric motor”, settle it.

R1 · [0030], [0061]

R1 · Original p. 1 R1 · Original p. 3

Limitation 1d · B 82

Kinoshita’s second propeller sits below the first on the same axis, the two turning in opposite directions; Pauli and Xing show the same unit, and Spirov and Pedersen give each of four ducted fans two counter-rotating units.

The reasons to combine are stated in the art (W2), but the owner has a colourable different-problem answer in Xing’s remark about multirotor balance.

R2 · Columns 3–4 R3 · Publication page 2

Limitation 1e · C 72

Kinoshita drives each propeller of the pair by its own variable-speed motor, the two rotating in opposite directions; Paul puts such counter-rotating electric motors in every one of three units.

No multi-unit reference calls the second motor speed-controlled; that comes from the single-pair references by the combination. A narrow open point.

R2 · Claim 1, column 5, lines 43–45

R2 · Columns 5–6

Limitation 1f · C 64

Within the pair, Kinoshita rotates each propeller at a speed independent of the other: both up or down to climb, a difference to turn. Across the craft, Louvel sets every motor’s speed separately.

A live open point: no reference varies the speeds of both motors of each of three or more coaxial units individually; one reference disclaims per-rotor speed control, another feeds both motors of a fan from one module.

R2 · Claim 1, column 5, lines 44–45 · R1 · [0061]

Limitation 1g · C 68

Louvel’s two crossing boards form four arms radiating from the central part, each arm end carrying one motor-propeller unit, with no member joining the motors.

Louvel’s solid casing side area is attached to the board ends, a ring tied to the arm ends. The patent’s own dependent claims add such protection rings to the same frame, so the clause excludes drive units secured to each other, not a guard on the arms. Narrow.

Read as the examiner and the patent read it, automatic control by programmable electronics, which Louvel’s sensor loop meets. Under the owner’s narrow reading, closed-loop attitude and position control on board, the chart rests on STARMAC’s on-board loops and on Wobben.

Claim-wide point W2 · B 80

The reason to fit Louvel’s quadrotor with the coaxial pair in which each rotor has its own speed-controlled electric motor: the art states it (torque cancelled within the pair, flight on one motor, more lift, more efficiency), and a 2004 four-unit craft took the step.

Counted once for the chart. The owner has a colourable answer: Xing’s remark that a multirotor’s balance is hard to control, and hindsight.
