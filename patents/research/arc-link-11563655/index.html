Software · Networking

Follow the virtual statistic to its physical counter.

Tenant isolation, shared physical resources and virtual port statistics.

The technical question

How did earlier network-virtualization systems provide tenant-specific statistics when physical resources were shared?

The research approach

The research connected OpenVirteX implementation history, technical papers, standards and patent disclosures. The chart distinguishes a source’s express teaching from the inference used in a combination.

Limitation 1b · C 60 · Weakest

Determine whether the resource is shared, and provide statistics accordingly.

The hypervisor already keeps, per physical port, the tenants whose virtual ports map to it. The Delftvisor note says a tenant’s port statistics on a shared link must be isolated by aggregating its own flows; the ICC 2012 paper states the same computation for networks sharing links.

Passages on the original page

Port statistics can be tricky since on shared links the traffic caused by each slices would need to be isolated. This could be done by remembering which flows cause packets to be forwarded to a port and query their statistics instead and aggregate those.

R2 · Delftvisor design note (TU Delft) · Delftvisor, design/design.md, lines 372–373 (GitHub, commit b1226578)

What the score reflects

The weakest limitation. No reference before the critical date performs the determination inside a network hypervisor; the two-branch rule rests on the design note, the paper and the mapping OpenVirteX holds. Scored at W1.

Limitation 1a · A 94

When a tenant controller sends a monitoring request, the hypervisor answers with translated monitoring information for that virtual network; OpenVirteX’s statistics code takes each tenant’s flow and port requests by its tenant id.

Literal. The owner can argue that a port or flow counter is not “individual physical resource consumption statistics”; the patent’s own usage settles it. Scored at W1.

Limitation 1c · A 94

The module gives the tenant both its virtual flow-entry statistics and its virtual port statistics, each from the hypervisor’s statistics replies.

The conjunction of 1c1 and 1c2, rated at the lower of the two. Scored at W1.

Limitation 1c1 · A 95

The hypervisor answers a tenant’s flow-statistics request with the counters of that tenant’s own entries, re-virtualized to the tenant’s cookie and match; Flo-v describes the same reply over the physical switches mapped to the requested virtual switch.

Literal, at the cap W1 sets.

Limitation 1c2 · A 94

A virtual port’s statistics are the mapped physical port’s counters renumbered to the virtual port; on a shared port the design note and the ICC 2012 paper derive them from the flow entries forwarding to that port.

Literal. The owner can argue that “based on the at least one physical resource” asks for the shared-branch computation; the patent’s own two branches settle it. Scored at W1.

Claim-wide point W2 · A 97

The OpenVirteX papers, its 2014 documentation captures and its source code describe one hypervisor; Flo-v and a 2017 measurement paper describe its statistics handling in print, and the patent names OpenVirteX as its prior art.
